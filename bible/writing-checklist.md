# Writing Checklist

Check these before planning a chapter and again before sending it.

*Merged from this novel's own checklist and the author's checklist for
another novel, adapted to this one. Lines that only fit the other novel are
dropped. Notes in italics are the author's reads of this novel's drafts, or
measurements taken when this version was adopted.*

## How to edit

- **Rebuild, don't patch.** When a scene needs changing, rework the whole
  scene and its structure, not a few added or swapped lines. If a new fact
  changes what matters in a loop or chapter (who a character cares about,
  what drives the plot), rebuild everything around it so it drives the
  scenes, and cut or replace whatever no longer fits. *The author's read of
  Loop 3: Eros and Seele were the most important things to Bronya, and the
  draft didn't show it.*
- **The plan isn't sacred.** Delete or rewrite a plan section, or the whole
  plan, when the story needs it. Update the plan, profiles and tracker after
  the rebuild so they match the chapter.
- Before a big rebuild, say what will change and why.

## Point of view

- **First person, past tense, told by Eros as "I".** The story can switch
  to another character's point of view when a scene needs something he
  can't see: a heroine's echoes, Theresa in her office, the island after he
  leaves it. Those sections are in close third person, past tense, and open
  on the character's name, so Eros is the only "I". In every section,
  nothing is on the page that its point-of-view character didn't see, hear,
  feel or work out.
- **The System is heard only in Eros's sections.** Nobody else hears it,
  and no other section quotes it.
- **His thoughts in the moment are in italics, and every scene has them.**
  Italics are also how he talks to the System in his head, because it hears
  them ([formatting.md](formatting.md)). Those lines are speech and follow
  the dialogue rules. The rest are him thinking.
- **A thought is him thinking, not an answer.** He works out where and when
  he is, plans the next minute, weighs a choice, counts, catches himself,
  remembers. A thought is never a silent comeback to what a person in the
  scene just said. A reply to the System is fine, because that's a
  conversation.
- **In his own sections the narration never names him.** Everyone calls him
  Eros, in every life, and each birth says how he got the name.
- **Every point-of-view character thinks in italics,** by the same rule:
  real thinking, never comebacks.

*Measured when this version was adopted: italics per chapter were 0
(Chapter 1), 4 (2), 20 (3), 14 (4) and 3 (5), nearly all of them lines to
the System.*

## Talk, not explanation

*Measured when this version was adopted: the share of words spoken was 12%
(Chapter 1), 18% (2), 18% (3), 18% (4) and 15% (5). Every chapter had ten or
more stretches of over 250 words with nobody speaking (Chapter 2 had 42),
and the longest ran to 1,638. By the measure below, every chapter so far is
being told. The author's read of Loop 4: "It's just Eros saying one sentence
and then her meters going up."*

- **If two people share a scene, the scene happens in their talk:** what
  they say, what they won't say, and what they do while they say it. The
  narration carries what Eros sees and does, not what it all means.
- **The narrator never says what a person is like or what a moment means.**
  If deleting the sentence leaves the scene working, it stays deleted.
  Loop 4 had "I'd put two true things next to each other and left her to do
  the rest, because she was cleverer than I was and would do it better."
- **Flashbacks are scenes too.** A memory that matters gets a place, its
  people and their lines. A childhood fork that comes back after 18 comes
  back as a scene, not as a recap of the log.
- **Summary only crosses time.** A few lines get from one scene to the
  next. If something changes in those months, it's a scene (see "No summary
  montages").
- **Nobody exists to explain.** A classmate who tells Eros what the
  Lightning Queen is, or a Valkyrie who explains a stigma, is the narrator
  in a coat. A line of explanation has to be something that person would
  say to that listener, then, for a reason of their own.
- **Lines sound spoken, not written.** People run on, double back, change
  their minds halfway, trail off, talk over each other and answer the
  question they wanted to be asked. Not every line is short, and not every
  line lands a point. Each person talks at the length their profile gives
  them: Kiana runs on with her mouth full, Mei finishes every sentence,
  Bronya reports, Tanabe says two formal words. When someone cares about a
  thing, let them talk about it for a while.
- **Eros talks.** In every scene he shares with someone, he speaks, and at
  least one of his lines is him working that person: asking, bargaining,
  needling or steering. He talks to get things, and most of it is questions
  about them. He has two voices: polite and harmless-looking with anyone who
  matters (colleagues, teachers, the heroines' families), and the dry
  engineer in his head and with the System ([his profile](characters/eros.md)).
  A shared scene where he says nothing needs a reason on the page, like the
  twenty minutes on the steps after the Kobe match.
- **The world arrives in use.** Stigmata, Honkai Resistance, the anomaly
  watch, the System's meters: people use these things and mention them in
  passing. Nobody stops to define them.
- **Measure it before sending.** Count the words inside quotation marks and
  the System's brackets. About a third is a healthy guide, and under a fifth
  means the chapter is being told. Find every stretch of more than about 250
  words with nobody speaking, and give each one a reason or a conversation.
  Run logs in code blocks don't count either way.

## Lines do something

- **Every line is an act.** Someone asks, begs, pushes, refuses, accuses,
  teases, comforts, dodges, bargains, warns, boasts or lies. A line that
  only says what happened, or what something looks like, is narration in
  quotation marks.
- **Nobody tells someone what they both know.** If a speaker wants the
  listener to know they know, they use it.
- **Nobody describes what's in front of them, or what the reader just
  read.**
- **Facts come bent by what the speaker wants.** Someone who has to give
  information gives it for a reason of their own, with their feeling in it,
  out of order, and only as much as they're pushed for. Rin tells him about
  the dawn practice because taping her wrist is the only time anyone in the
  hall sits still. A girl whose father was arrested on Saturday doesn't
  recite her weekend to a classmate.
- **Grief talks around the thing.** People talk about the bento, the shoes
  in the bin, the empty seat at the match. The loss is in what they won't
  say.
- **Describing is the narration's job.** What a Herrscher looks like, what
  someone did and how a stigma works are shown in the narration, or slip
  out inside an act, as a warning, a boast or a complaint.
- **The test:** if a line could go into the narration as "she told me
  that..." and lose nothing, it's a report. Make it an act, or cut it.
- **Measure it before sending.** Mark every line that only reports or
  describes. Keep it under about one line in ten, each with a reason.

## Voices

*The author's read: the heroines' personalities and speech weren't theirs
until the canon research went into their profiles. And Seele asked Eros to
go into the experiment in her place, which Seele would never do. She's a
good kid.*

- **Canon characters talk by their voice sheets.** Each canon character's
  profile has one, built from the research: register, names, how she sounds
  angry, sad or teasing, sample lines, what to avoid. Check every line of
  hers against it.
- **A person's words fit their own life.** What she says comes from what
  she's lived, in canon and in this file: Bronya's reports in the third
  person, Kiana's food and her "stinky old man", Mei's formal Japanese with
  anyone she doesn't trust.
- **Signature lines can come back across loops.** A catchphrase from her
  voice sheet is her voice, not a replay ("I'm sorry. No."). Everything else
  in a replayed moment follows "Lives that repeat".

## His knowledge

*The author's read of Loop 1: "You had Eros spill info again, stop with this
stupid thing." And of Chapter 4: "I'd never once seen her lose" was false.*

- **He uses what he knows, and it shows.** Every chapter has moments where
  he acts on something only he knows: he's in the right place first,
  prepares before it happens, steers someone without telling them why,
  makes or saves money, or steps around a trap. The reader sees the
  knowledge go in and the result come out, good or bad.
- **Never on the heroine.** In a heroine run his game knowledge decides
  where he stands and when, never what he says to her. What he sees in the
  file, he can give her. What he knows from the game, he keeps. Nobody hears
  anything from him that he couldn't have seen.
- **He collects on purpose.** He's a records man. He asks questions nobody
  else asks, reads files and boards, times and counts things, checks what
  changed from the game and from the last file, and keeps it in the coded
  timeline and in his head for the next run. Show him doing it, and show
  what he gets.
- **He cashes it in.** What he collects becomes money, position and power
  on the page: the anomaly watch that made his name, the run-away fund, a
  debt held over someone, a rumor put in the right ear. Show the deal and
  what it buys him.
- **Show the gap.** Someone says the wrong thing with confidence, and he
  knows better and keeps quiet, or he knows better and it costs him to say
  so.
- **His knowledge has edges.** He knows HI3 as a player knew it: the main
  story, the manga, the events he played, the forums. He doesn't know what
  the game never showed, and this world isn't the game (its ages, its faces,
  its fashion, the column of things this world does its own way). Inside a
  run he knows earlier files only as far as his soul stage lets him
  ([soul-and-fate.md](soul-and-fate.md)). His foreknowledge is never lost.
- **The world gives him plenty to know.** Canon dates and this story's
  changes are in the [timeline](timeline.md), so every chapter has events
  he can see coming.
- **No "I knew" litanies.** Never "I knew X. I knew Y. I knew Z." Show what
  he knows through one concrete, present detail ("Her daughter was fifteen
  that autumn, in an orphanage in Sochi"). "I knew" about 30 times a chapter
  at most.
- **No coy hints.** Say the thing ("Cocolia's name") or leave it out; no
  "what had been taken from her, and what had been done with what was left".
- **No recognition tic.** Don't open with "I knew that voice / face / ship /
  line." Go straight to the memory, at most twice a chapter.
- **Don't reuse a memory.** A first-life memory (crying over Kallen in the
  bathroom at work, putting the phone down at a cutscene) appears once.
- **Measure it.** Before sending, list each scene's knowledge used and
  knowledge gained. A scene with neither has him just living.

## His hunger for women

*The author's read: the comments sounded like a third-rate gang member, and
for a while the vulgarity was diluting the story. Keep the clothes and the
hunger; give the comments his own voice.*

- **Show it every chapter.** His eyes go to adult women, and the narration
  says so plainly, for a line or two, then gets on with the scene.
- **In his own voice.** Dry, specific, a fan's and an engineer's eye: the
  fan-art folder, the banner at three in the morning, patch notes, crash
  logs, a screenshot before a crash. Blunt words are rare so they land. It
  changes with his soul: eager and half in love in Loop 1, taking stock in
  the middle loops, flat and clinical when Torn ("the one process still
  running at full speed").
- **It's his hunger, not a joke.** It comes from the same place as his
  hunger for power (strong women who belong to him are proof of it), and
  he's just as calculating about it.
- **It almost never gets out of his mouth.** Looking harmless is the plan,
  and his face stays clean. The System hears all of it and answers it
  differently each time, never with the same joke.
- **As vulgar as his voice gets, never explicit.** No sex is written out,
  and the scene cuts before anything happens. Adults only: see "Content".

## Lives that repeat

- **The world replays; the page doesn't.** The reader has lived every
  earlier file with Eros. Whatever happens the same way as before gets a
  line of summary, like crossing time ("I let the morning run, and every
  word came back"). Only what's different is written as a scene. A
  childhood log can say "The same as the fourth file" and pick up where it
  changes.
- **No dialogue is copied from an earlier loop or chapter.** If a known
  line has to show, give its first words, or let Eros say it before the
  speaker can. A voice-sheet catchphrase is the exception (see "Voices").
- **Use the loop's own clichés.** He finishes someone's line for them, or
  knows what comes next and moves first, and the speaker stares. That's
  new, and the reader enjoys knowing what he knows. Never on the heroine
  where it would spill.
- **Start a replayed scene at the first change.**
- **A replayed moment shows something new,** something he missed the
  first time. If it shows nothing new, cut it.
- **Memories come from what earlier chapters skipped,** never a retelling
  of their scenes. A scene the reader has read gets one image at most: the
  click of Bronya's legs, not the terrace.
- **Every loop opens with what the reset erased.** In the white, before he
  plans anything, he registers what the death took from his soul and what
  went with the file: the people, promises and things that are gone.
- **Every chapter has a moment where being the only one who remembers
  costs him:** a question he can't answer truthfully, or grief for someone
  who's standing in front of him, like Mrs. Okada on the stairs with the
  paper boat.
- **Measure it.** Before sending, count the lines of dialogue that match an
  earlier chapter word for word, and the paragraphs that retell one. Aim
  for none.

## Life Path and heroine runs

*The author's reads: after 18 it should be immersive, not System logs; the
deaths need the actual scenes; and of a Loop 3 summary paragraph, "Again?
Didn't I tell you to write actual scenes?"*

- **After 18 it's immersive.** Once Eros takes over at 18, everything is
  written as scenes with dialogue. No dated log lines. Only the System's
  status windows (meters, notices) stay in boxes, and the System's
  questions are asked out loud.
- **No summary montages.** Anything that matters after 18 (a blow landing
  on her, a meter moving, a choice) is a scene with people in a place,
  talking, not a paragraph that lists what happened ("A reporter got a
  tip. A parent got a phone call. The form was lost twice."). If it's worth
  naming, it's worth showing.
- **Every death is a full scene.** Show the death itself, the moment and
  what it feels like, through to the end. Never cut away just before it or
  sum it up in a log line. A death inside a childhood log plays out as a
  full scene when the memory comes back in the white.
- **Forks come back.** Every childhood fork has a consequence that returns
  later in that life. Nothing romantic or sexual happens before 18, and no
  fork completes a hidden objective.
- **Each meter step takes several scenes,** in this order: what Eros tries;
  how she resists, tests him or goes her own way; something that backfires
  or costs him; something in her own life he doesn't control; and only
  then the shift.
- **A meter moves only on something she saw and could read.** *The
  author's read of Loop 1: her feelings rose while she was still the
  Lightning Queen, for nothing but his confessions.*
- **She always wants something of her own** and acts on it. Eros has to
  work round her, not just talk at her.
- **At least one failed move per stage.** Corruption is work he's doing.
- **Meter windows only at real thresholds,** four or five per loop. Between
  them, the change shows in what she does: saving a seat, waiting at the
  gate, flinching when he's kind, not asking where he's been.
- **Ordinary days.** Some scenes don't turn anything. A lunch where nothing
  happens. A walk in the rain. They're what make the turns feel earned.
- **Check the objectives.** Every loop gets a table against the heroine's
  hidden objectives. Once one completes, his next death ends the run.

## Logic

- **Everything has a reason.** Before a character does something, know why
  they do it at that moment. If the only reason is that the plot needs it,
  change it. *The author's read of the first Loop 1: "Why did Eros do such
  stupid moves? Why didn't the fan in him start confessing to her every
  day?"*
- **Cause comes first, on the page.** What makes a thing happen is shown
  before the thing.
- **No info dumps.** Give the reader a fact when a scene needs it, through
  what people do and say. No paragraph stops to explain the world or
  somebody's past.
- **Eros makes the turn.** In each scene, find the choice that turns it.
  If someone else always makes it, rewrite. The heroine still wants things
  and acts on them; he has to answer her.
- **Changes in Eros happen on the page.** When he changes how he plays
  (warm to cold, a new method, a line he swore he wouldn't cross), show the
  moment he decides, with its reason, as a scene in the white or in the
  loop. One sentence isn't a turn, and neither is the next loop just
  starting different. The reason has to fit his soul stage at that point.
  *The author's read: "Why did Eros start becoming cold in Loop 4?"*
- **A person's words fit their own life.** See "Voices".
- **Institutions act like real ones.** Schicksal, Anti-Entropy, ME Corp, the
  police, the receiver, a school board, a bank: each acts as a real one
  would, through the people a real one would send. If it doesn't, the
  chapter shows why. *The author's read of the first Loop 3: Cocolia sent
  Bronya to kill Eros. Why would she kill one of her own children, and why
  send Bronya, when she has people with guns for a normal human? And the
  gray man wouldn't touch her favorite unless he wanted to die too.*

## What people know

- **Everyone reacts with what they know.** A reaction comes from what that
  person has seen and heard in this life, and from who they are. Nobody
  knows about the System, so when Eros does something that only makes
  sense with his memories, people read it with the nearest ordinary thing
  they have: nerves, a crush, ambition, grief.
- **People act only on what they know.** Before anyone acts, find where
  they learned what they act on, on the page or in their past. Nobody acts
  on what the reader knows, what the bible says, or what Eros remembers
  from another file. A guess is fine when the person has a reason to make
  it, and a guess can be wrong.
- **The most anyone guesses is a spy or a genius.** When Eros knows too
  much, people read it as a brilliant model, luck, eavesdropping, a
  stalker's notebook ("You've written a page on me"), or somebody's agent
  (Brenner's file, Rita's report to Otto). Nobody guesses the System,
  another life or a game.
- **Heroines remember other files only as echoes,** and echoes feel like
  dreams, not facts ([simulation-system.md](simulation-system.md)).

## Power, rank and the Honkai

- **Rank follows the scale.** Check every Valkyrie's rank against
  [power-scale.md](power-scale.md). S-ranks are one or two at a time at
  most: Theresa in 2009, and Durandal and Rita not yet.
- **Resistance is a ceiling.** Check every body against its Honkai
  Resistance grade. F is a normal human with a Peak Human ceiling; D gives
  hours in Eruption air. Eros is Civilian, and the Low D gift in Mei's run
  keeps him breathing on the roof stairs. It doesn't let him fight.
- **Power follows rank.** What a rank can do and what it breaks is in the
  same file. Read the row before writing a fight or a show of power.
- **Nothing above the ceiling without a cost.** A push past a body's grade
  risks the cascade. A Herrscher's authority is a class of its own.
- **Beasts by class.** Honkai beasts match [honkai-beasts.md](honkai-beasts.md).
- **Readings match the scale.** The watch room's numbers mean what they
  meant before: 1,184 HW is a Herrscher.
- **Fights are settled by what's on the page:** rank, gear, numbers,
  preparation and ground. Know who wins and why before the fight starts,
  and put the why on the page first. Eros survives by preparation, gifts
  and running, never by out-fighting a rank above him.

## Technology and money

- **Every scene shows the technology its people would have, in use.** A
  phone in a hand, a screen in the watch room, a badge at the canteen till,
  a message at the wrong moment, a car at the curb. In a run it's that year
  and place's: landlines in Krakow in 1987, a lamp in Hokkaido in 1908.
- **The HI3 world is ahead of Earth, and the gap grows**
  ([far-east-branch.md](far-east-branch.md)): about Earth's 2014 in 2009,
  Earth's 2017 by 2011, light science fiction by 2014. Schicksal is further
  ahead again, and deliberately ordinary in the staff's daily life.
- **Nobody explains it.** It's as ordinary to them as ours is to us.
- **News travels by it.** Alerts, messages, the watch room's feeds, the
  television in the common room.
- **People apart talk by phone.** Ryoma's calls, Bronya's Sunday call at
  nine, Tanabe ringing every evening.
- **It stays behind the Honkai.** The phone is in the background; the
  Honkai, the stigmata and the System are in front.
- **Earth's culture is here, in this world's versions:** games, web novels,
  streams, films, music, memes. Honkai Impact itself isn't, and nobody here
  has played it. His gamer words (bug, patch, save file, banner) are
  ordinary words; the story he knows is his alone.
- **Prices in the local money of that time and place:** yen in Nagazora,
  euros on the island, yuan in Hangzhou, kronor in Malmö, złoty in Krakow,
  rubles in Siberia, at that year's real prices. Check every price. Nobody
  invents a currency except the System, whose Fate Fragments are in
  [gifts-and-rewards.md](gifts-and-rewards.md).
- **Measure it before sending.** List the technology in each scene. A scene
  with people in it and nothing on the list gets something, unless the
  place has none.

## Gifts on the page

*The author's read: "Did you forget Eros's gifts and skills from previous
chapters?"*

- Each gift he holds shows up at least once in a chapter where it matters,
  through something he does, and with its limit showing too. Sprinter wins
  the first ten meters and loses the next hundred. Quick Reflexes catches
  the falling cup and makes him flinch before the punch. Quick Learner gets
  the footwork in a week while his lungs still give out. Permanent gifts
  come into every loop with him, in a child's body too.
- The narration doesn't name the gift ("thanks to Quick Learner"). The log
  lines can, in brackets.
- A gift does one thing. Don't let it quietly cover things outside its
  effect in [gift-catalog.md](gift-catalog.md).

## Valkyries on the page

- **Every scene with a Valkyrie shows what makes her one:** a stigma, a
  weapon or a suit, a reflex no ordinary woman has, how the Honkai sits on
  her body.
- **The strong keep it in.** An A-rank doesn't show off in a canteen. When
  an S-rank's power comes out, it's an event.
- **Men with Honkai in them are rare,** and people notice. A Knight is
  something to stare at.
- **Legends are deeds, not stands.** A Valkyrie's story is something she
  killed, broke, burned or saved: Theresa killing Parvati alone. No
  legend is someone holding a gate, a line or a door until dawn. The author
  has banned it twice.
- **Measure it before sending.** List every Valkyrie in each scene and what
  shows of her power.

## The Honkai in daily life

- **Every chapter shows the Honkai in ordinary life:** a memorial for the
  Second Eruption, a news item with a closed zone, a case of Honkai
  sickness, a cover story, a drill, a detector on a mast. And at least one
  piece of faith: a shrine at New Year, a prayer at a bedside, Schicksal's
  chapel and its saints, a grandmother's icon.
- **Faith and fear change what people do.** A mother won't let her son near
  the harbor, a fisherman touches a charm, Schicksal hides behind a wind
  research station. It shows in what they do, not in explanations.
- **Schicksal looks and sounds like a church** with an army inside it: its
  Archbishop, its saints, the habits at St. Freya, its oaths.
- **The Honkai is rarely seen, and never casual.** When a Herrscher wakes
  or an Eruption comes, the sky and the city show it, and the scene treats
  it as the biggest thing in it.
- **Measure it before sending.** List where the chapter shows the Honkai
  and faith. A chapter with none gets one.

## Continuity

- **Nobody quotes a scene that isn't on the page.** Before a character
  refers back to something ("you told me last week"), find that scene in an
  earlier chapter.
- **Track who knows what.** Who knows about the System (nobody, so far),
  what Eros has told whom, what Schicksal knows about him, and which
  heroines have echoes or memories of him.
- **Track who holds what.** Items, gear, money and documents, plus the
  System's side: permanent gifts, Fate Fragments, soul stage. Keep
  [tracker.md](tracker.md) current after each chapter.
- **Injuries and fatigue have causes and last.**
- **Names, dates, ranks and numbers match earlier chapters and the bible.**
- **Do the sums.** Check every price, wage, reward and count against the
  others and against the bible: his salary (€78,000, then €140,000 from
  2012), Fate Fragments in and out, readings, distances, the ferry's days.
- **Date every scene before drafting.** List each scene's date and weekday
  and every named person's age at it, and whether each woman on the page is
  18 yet. *Earlier drafts of Loop 4 swapped a Wednesday and a Thursday, sent
  Mei to the lawyer on the wrong day and put December out of order.*
- **Read it once for contradictions.** *An earlier Loop 4 had Mei believe
  a forged number when Tanabe's real one was already in her call log.*
- **Check canon before using it,** against sources, and put the links in
  the plan. Mark story changes as story changes. If the research is thin,
  write the author a search prompt instead of guessing. *The author's
  reads: Mr. HOMU wears a full yellow Homu suit, not a white mask; Bronya's
  legs were fine until Sin Mal broke them, and the experiment took her
  emotions, which only Seele (and Eros, in that run) could bring back.*
- **The bible moves with the chapter.** A chapter isn't finished until the
  timeline, the profiles, the plan and the tracker match it.

## Names

- **No name arrives bare.** The first time a person, place, group or term
  appears in the book, the same sentence or the next one says who or what
  it is, in a few words, as Eros would say it: "a careful girl called
  Hayashi Rin who looked like she spent a lot of her life apologizing."
- **That includes names inside dialogue.** If a character says a name the
  reader hasn't met, the speaker or the narration explains it right there.
- **One short clause is enough.** An introduction is not an info dump.
- **Names from earlier chapters aren't introduced again.** They get a short
  tag at most, and only if they haven't come up for a while.
- **Before sending, list every name in the chapter** and find the line where
  each one is introduced, in this chapter or an earlier one. A name with no
  such line gets one or gets cut.

## Dialogue logic

- Every line follows from what the speaker knows and wants at that moment.
- **No misplaced blame.** Characters blame the person who did it, not
  whoever is standing next to them, unless the scene is about that
  unfairness and treats it as such.
- **Prices are clear:** who's paying, for what, and who named it.
- **Disclosures are earned.** A guarded character doesn't confide in one
  exchange. Each private thing she gives costs her something and fits what
  she knows of him by then. Most of what Eros learns about a guarded
  heroine, he learns by watching. If she would never tell him, he finds out
  another way, or not at all. *The author's read of Chapter 4: Mei told
  her secrets to a stranger over one shared sentence.*
- **Lies have to survive the listener.** Mei reads words, not faces, so
  Liar's Face only hides his face. A lie that works on her is built from
  true things, or is too short to read. Check every lie against how that
  character actually tests things.
- **Subtext.** Nobody says their fear, their wish or the theme out loud.
  They talk about the bento or the match, and the feeling shows in what
  they avoid and what their hands do. If a line could come straight from
  their profile, rewrite it.

## Staging

- **Tone shifts get staged.** When a character switches register (stern to
  playful, polite to cold, a joke, a catchphrase, a pose, a burst of
  anger), show it in the body and the voice: what the face does, what the
  hands do, how the pitch or speed changes, and how the people around
  react. One or two concrete details, before or after the line.
- **Signature moments get their own beat.** A character's catchphrase,
  habit or canon mannerism doesn't get buried inside a paragraph. Give it
  room, and let Eros the fan recognize it when he would.
- **The flat-voice test.** If a line that carries a change of mood would
  read the same in a flat voice, it needs staging.

## Feeling

- **One feeling per scene, and neighbouring scenes don't share it.** Each
  big feeling comes out at full strength once a chapter. Elsewhere he holds
  it in, or it leaks out sideways.
- **Every big event gets its aftermath** (scene and sequel, from Dwight
  Swain): his reaction, then a dilemma with no good option, then what he
  decides, which becomes the next scene's goal. It can be three lines or a
  conversation, but it's on the page before the next scene starts. After a
  death, the white is that aftermath.

## Big moments and crowds

*When something happens in front of people (an assembly, an entrance, an
Eruption, a reading on the big screen), the people are part of the event.*

- **Show the crowd reacting.** Don't sum it up in one line like "the hall
  erupted."
- **The key fact lands first.** Then a beat of silence if it's a shock. Then
  the noise.
- **Start with what Eros would notice first.**
- **Pick a few single people out of the crowd,** and give each a different,
  specific reaction that fits who they are: the proud one, the rival, the
  one who stands to gain, the one who's just been outdone.
- **Say where the noise starts** and what it sounds like. A crowd isn't one
  voice.
- **Don't stack them.** About six single people at most, plus the crowd's
  shouted lines, then move on.
- **End on a consequence,** not on another reaction: somebody leaves to make
  a call, somebody runs for the stairs.

## Cliché, on purpose

*Big moments are done the anime and gacha way, loud and familiar. Don't
understate them, and don't swap the stock beat for a subtle one.*

- **When a moment has a stock anime version, write that version:** a
  Herrscher waking, a Valkyrie's entrance, the transfer student at the front
  of the class, power shown, a rival outdone, a rescue at the last second,
  the ship's lights coming over the roof.
- **Power is a physical event.** Light that leaves its source, wind
  indoors, a sound, things that bend, break or go out. Size it to the rank
  ([power-scale.md](power-scale.md)), so the bigger ranks still have
  somewhere to go.
- **The crowd says it out loud.** The rank, "impossible", how rare it is,
  who she is. Short shouted lines from different voices. ("That's a
  Herrscher.")
- **Stock reactions are welcome.** The rival clicks his tongue, the old hand
  smiles and says nothing, the reading gets its own gasp.
- **The rest of this checklist still applies.** Each reaction fits who the
  person is, the key fact lands first, the scene ends on a consequence, and
  the prose check still runs.

## Rooms with regulars

*The places he keeps coming back to are the novel's stock hubs: the island's
watch room on the ninth floor, its canteen and the seawall, and in a
heroine run the class and the club (in Mei's run, 3-A and the kendo hall).*

- **The regulars are on the page by name.** Every scene in one of these
  rooms puts at least four of them in it, each busy with something of their
  own (eating, arguing, mending, betting, gossiping), each with the habit
  that makes them easy to spot.
- **The room reacts.** When something happens there (a reading on the big
  screen, a scholarship read out, a word on a desk), the room takes it as a
  crowd does, by "Big moments and crowds".
- **The room talks.** Rumors land there first: what the watch caught last
  night, who's being sent where, whose father was on the news.
- **It changes with the years:** people are promoted, transferred, married,
  hurt and killed, and new faces come every September.
- **Measure it before sending.** List who's on the page in each such scene
  and what each one is doing. Fewer than four, or anyone just standing
  there, gets fixed.

## Content

- **Adults only, by each woman's own birthday.** Anything sexual, and the
  world's fashion, is for women of 18 and over on the day of the scene.
  Girls under 18 wear the ordinary uniform and are never described in the
  fashion ([world-fashion.md](world-fashion.md),
  [content-rules.md](content-rules.md)).
- **Nothing in a childhood log:** nothing romantic or sexual, and nobody's
  clothes described in that way.
- **Adults wear the world's fashion,** vulgar and kinky, private areas
  covered, no micro bikinis.
- **No sex written out,** and no sexual coercion. It happens off the page,
  between adults.

## Before each chapter

- New characters get profiles in [characters/](characters/) before the
  chapter. The more important the character, the more detail.
- **List every person who is named or speaks, and find their profile.** A
  crowd voice with one shouted line is the crowd.
- A chapter after a time skip opens by showing where things stand: the
  status panel and what the skip cost.
- Dark scenes are built fresh for the chapter they're in.
- **Each scene in the plan gets three notes:** whether it's new or a
  replay, its one feeling, and its aftermath if something big happens in
  it.
- **Every plan has two fixed beats:** what the reset erased, for every loop
  and run, and the moment where being the only one who remembers costs him.
- **Reread a few pages of the last chapter the author approved** before
  drafting, and match its rhythm.
- **Draft scene by scene.** Write one or two scenes, check them against
  this list, then go on.

## Prose check (lint before sending)

- No em or en dashes.
- No colons in the narration.
- "The way" about 25 times a chapter at most. "I knew" about 30.
- ♪ three times a chapter at most.
- No "It wasn't X. It was Y." or other not-this-but-that lines.
- **No near-lie.** A character weighs a lie, lists what he could have said,
  then tells the truth ("I thought about lying... I could have said...
  'Yes,' I said"). It makes the truth read like a hidden good heart. If
  Eros tells the truth, it's a move: show the cold reason in a line, or
  nothing at all. If he lies, he just lies, and the narration doesn't
  announce it. The same goes for every "I could have X. I didn't." beat
  that ends on the softer choice.
- No paragraph or chapter that ends on a dramatic one-liner or a neat
  little moral.
- **Count the similes** ("like a", "as if", "as a man", "as you'd"). Keep
  the ones that do work, cut the rest, and never use one twice. "For a long
  time" stays rare too.

Before sending, also:

- **Measure the talk:** the share of words spoken and every silent stretch
  over about 250 words (see "Talk, not explanation").
- **List every line where someone states a feeling or the theme,** then
  rewrite or cut each one.
- **List every narrator explanation** ("which was the thing about...",
  "she was cleverer than I was and would do it better") and cut them.
- **Check the feelings scene by scene,** so no two neighbours repeat.
- **Check the fixed beats:** every big event has its aftermath, every loop
  opens on what the reset erased, and every chapter has its moment of
  isolation.
- **Count the dialogue copied from an earlier chapter** (see "Lives that
  repeat"). It should be none.
- **List the technology in each scene,** and check every price (see
  "Technology and money").
- **List every Valkyrie in each scene and what shows of her power,** and
  where the chapter shows the Honkai and faith.
- **Check every woman's age on the day of each scene** (see "Content").
- **Read the dialogue aloud.** If most lines are under about ten words, or
  every line lands a point, loosen them.
- **Mark every line that only reports or describes,** and make each one an
  act or cut it (see "Lines do something").

Also avoid:

- Narrator asides like "I want to be honest about this" or "Later, I would
  learn".
- Piles of similes, "the way you..." comparisons and poetic metaphors.
- Mini-essays about society or human nature.
- Lists of three for rhythm ("Still X. Still Y. Still Z.").
- Repeating one word until it turns into a symbol.
- Explaining what a moment means right after showing it.

# Writing Checklist

Check these before planning a chapter and again before sending it.

## Continuity

- **Nobody quotes a scene that isn't on the page.** Before a character
  refers back to something ("you told me last week"), find that scene in an
  earlier chapter.
- **Track who knows what.** Who knows about the System (nobody, so far),
  what Eros has told whom, what Schicksal knows about him, and which
  heroines have echoes or memories of him.
- **Track who holds what.** Items, gear and documents, plus the System's
  side: gifts in slots, Fate Fragments, soul stage. Keep
  [tracker.md](tracker.md) current after each chapter.
- **Injuries and fatigue have causes and last.**
- **Names, dates, ranks and numbers match earlier chapters and the bible.**

## Dialogue logic

- Every line follows from what the speaker knows and wants at that moment.
- **No misplaced blame.** Characters blame the person who did it, not
  whoever is standing next to them, unless the scene is about that
  unfairness and treats it as such.
- **Prices are clear:** who's paying, for what, and who named it.

## Before each chapter

- New characters get profiles in [characters/](characters/) before the
  chapter. The more important the character, the more detail.
- A chapter after a time skip opens by showing where things stand: the
  status panel and what the skip cost.
- Dark scenes are built fresh for the chapter they're in.

## Prose check (lint before sending)

- No em or en dashes.
- No colons in the narration.
- "The way" about 25 times a chapter at most.
- No "It wasn't X. It was Y." or other not-this-but-that lines.
- **No near-lie.** A character weighs a lie, lists what he could have said,
  then tells the truth ("I thought about lying... I could have said...
  'Yes,' I said"). It makes the truth read like a hidden good heart. If
  Eros tells the truth, it's a move: show the cold reason in a line, or
  nothing at all. If he lies, he just lies, and the narration doesn't
  announce it. The same goes for every "I could have X. I didn't." beat
  that ends on the softer choice.
- No paragraph or chapter that ends on a dramatic one-liner or a neat
  little moral.

Also avoid:

- Narrator asides like "I want to be honest about this" or "Later, I would
  learn".
- Piles of similes, "the way you..." comparisons and poetic metaphors.
- Mini-essays about society or human nature.
- Lists of three for rhythm ("Still X. Still Y. Still Z.").
- Repeating one word until it turns into a symbol.
- Explaining what a moment means right after showing it.

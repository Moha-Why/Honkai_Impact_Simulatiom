# Writing Checklist

Check these before planning a chapter and again before sending it.

## How to edit

- **Rebuild, don't patch.** When a scene needs changing, rework the whole
  scene and its structure, not a few added or swapped lines. If a new fact
  changes what matters in a loop or chapter (who a character cares about,
  what drives the plot), rebuild everything around it so it drives the
  scenes, and cut or replace whatever no longer fits.
- **The plan isn't sacred.** Delete or rewrite a plan section, or the whole
  plan, when the story needs it. Update the plan, profiles and tracker after
  the rebuild so they match the chapter.
- Before a big rebuild, say what will change and why.

## Life Path runs

- **After 18 it's immersive.** Once Eros takes over at 18, everything is
  written as scenes with dialogue. No dated log lines. Only the System's
  status windows (meters, notices) stay in boxes, and the System's
  questions are asked out loud.
- **Every death is a full scene.** Show the death itself, the moment and
  what it feels like, through to the end. Never cut away just before it or
  sum it up in a log line. A death inside a childhood log plays out as a
  full scene when the memory comes back in the white.

## Continuity

- **Nobody quotes a scene that isn't on the page.** Before a character
  refers back to something ("you told me last week"), find that scene in an
  earlier chapter.
- **Track who knows what.** Who knows about the System (nobody, so far),
  what Eros has told whom, what Schicksal knows about him, and which
  heroines have echoes or memories of him.
- **Track who holds what.** Items, gear and documents, plus the System's
  side: permanent gifts, Fate Fragments, soul stage. Keep
  [tracker.md](tracker.md) current after each chapter.
- **Injuries and fatigue have causes and last.**
- **Names, dates, ranks and numbers match earlier chapters and the bible.**

## Dialogue logic

- Every line follows from what the speaker knows and wants at that moment.
- **No misplaced blame.** Characters blame the person who did it, not
  whoever is standing next to them, unless the scene is about that
  unfairness and treats it as such.
- **Prices are clear:** who's paying, for what, and who named it.

## Staging

- **Tone shifts get staged.** When a character switches register (stern to
  playful, polite to cold, a joke, a catchphrase, a pose, a burst of
  anger), show it in the body and the voice: what the face does, what the
  hands do, how the pitch or speed changes, and how the people around
  react. One or two concrete details, before or after the line.
- **Signature moments get their own beat.** A character's catchphrase,
  habit or canon mannerism doesn't get buried inside a paragraph. Give it
  room, and let Eros the fan recognize it when he would.
- **The flat-voice test.** If a line that carries a change of mood would
  read the same in a flat voice, it needs staging.

## Before each chapter

- New characters get profiles in [characters/](characters/) before the
  chapter. The more important the character, the more detail.
- A chapter after a time skip opens by showing where things stand: the
  status panel and what the skip cost.
- Dark scenes are built fresh for the chapter they're in.

## Eros's fan knowledge

- **No "I knew" litanies.** Never "I knew X. I knew Y. I knew Z." Show what
  he knows through one concrete, present detail ("Her daughter was fifteen
  that autumn, in an orphanage in Sochi"), not a list of hints.
- **No coy hints.** Say the thing ("Cocolia's name") or leave it out; no
  "what had been taken from her, and what had been done with what was left".
- **No recognition tic.** Don't open with "I knew that voice / face / ship /
  line." Go straight to the memory, at most twice a chapter.
- **Don't reuse a memory.** A first-life memory (crying over Kallen in the
  bathroom at work, putting the phone down at a cutscene) appears once.
- "I knew" about 30 times a chapter at most.

## Gifts on the page

- Each gift he holds shows up at least once in a chapter where it matters,
  through something he does, and with its limit showing too. Sprinter wins
  the first ten meters and loses the next hundred. Quick Reflexes catches
  the falling cup and makes him flinch before the punch. Quick Learner gets
  the footwork in a week while his lungs still give out.
- The narration doesn't name the gift ("thanks to Quick Learner"). The log
  lines can, in brackets.
- A gift does one thing. Don't let it quietly cover things outside its
  effect in [gift-catalog.md](gift-catalog.md).

## Prose check (lint before sending)

- No em or en dashes.
- No colons in the narration.
- "The way" about 25 times a chapter at most.
- No "It wasn't X. It was Y." or other not-this-but-that lines.
- **No near-lie.** A character weighs a lie, lists what he could have said,
  then tells the truth ("I thought about lying... I could have said...
  'Yes,' I said"). It makes the truth read like a hidden good heart. If
  Eros tells the truth, it's a move: show the cold reason in a line, or
  nothing at all. If he lies, he just lies, and the narration doesn't
  announce it. The same goes for every "I could have X. I didn't." beat
  that ends on the softer choice.
- No paragraph or chapter that ends on a dramatic one-liner or a neat
  little moral.

Also avoid:

- Narrator asides like "I want to be honest about this" or "Later, I would
  learn".
- Piles of similes, "the way you..." comparisons and poetic metaphors.
- Mini-essays about society or human nature.
- Lists of three for rhythm ("Still X. Still Y. Still Z.").
- Repeating one word until it turns into a symbol.
- Explaining what a moment means right after showing it.

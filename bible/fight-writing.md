# Fight Writing Guide

How fights are planned and written. Adapted from the fight guide in the
author's Novel repo (its anime style, its process and its hole checklist),
scaled to this story's Eros, who stays weak for a long time.

## 1. Process (in this order)

1. **Kits first.** Every fighter in the scene has a kit in
   [combat-kits.md](combat-kits.md): what they can do, what each move looks
   like, and what stops them. No moves made up on the spot.
2. **Beat plan.** Map the fight beat by beat (round by round for strong
   opponents): who does what, what it costs, where the danger is, where the
   impact frames go.
3. **The author sees the beat plan first.** Write the fight only after a
   yes.
4. **Write, run the hole checklist (section 5), apply, commit, push, send
   the file.** Then update what the fight leaves behind: injuries, ammunition,
   broken things, the tracker, and later scenes that refer back.

## 2. Anime style (the core)

- **Named moves.** A move gets a name when someone in the scene names it:
  - The System calls Eros's techniques in brackets, like a game screen:
    [Sprinter.] [Field Craft: Deer Cut.] It can also put up an enemy's big
    move when Eros sees it, like a boss attack banner.
  - Kiana shouts names she makes up on the spot, and the same move can get a
    new name every time ("Kaslana Special!").
  - A Herrscher names her big moves herself, in a word or two.
  - Names only appear where the point-of-view character could see or hear
    them. No System brackets in Kiana's or Mei's scenes.
  - A move name in the System's brackets may carry a colon ([Field Craft:
    Deer Cut.]). It's a label in the System's voice, not narration.
- **A clear visual and a sound for every move that matters.** Sound effects
  in italic capitals: *BANG*, *SHHK*, *KRAKA-THOOM*.
- **Impact frames on the big hits.** One frozen frame (where everyone is,
  what's in the air), then the delayed sound. Only for moves with real power
  behind them (Kiana's, a Herrscher's). Eros's own moves don't get one while
  he's weak: the System names them, they get a small sound, and that's all.
- **Spectacle at the right scale.** A box cutter drops a man to one knee;
  Kiana's guns blow out a row of pachinko machines; a Herrscher takes a roof
  apart. The destruction matches who's doing it.
- **Graphic, with aftermath.** Show what a blow does to a body and what's
  left: burns, a torn coat, bruises in the shape of fingers, blistered
  palms. Injuries carry into the next scenes.
- **Violent, not talky.** The feeling is in refusing to stop, not in
  speeches. Herrschers don't explain their hearts: a few words, commands and
  contempt, and the turn shows in what she does.
- **Onlookers between beats,** never in the middle of a climax.
- **The prose rules still apply** ([writing-checklist.md](writing-checklist.md)):
  plain words around the spectacle, no em dashes, no colons in narration, no
  explaining what the fight already showed.

## 3. Shape, scaled to the enemy

- **Eros acts.** In every fight he's in, he does something offensive he can
  actually do: one cut, one shot, one trick with the ground. He never only
  dodges and waits to be rescued. He's allowed to fail at it.
- **Match the shape to the threat:**
  - **Fodder** (one or a few turned people): short and brutal. No rounds, no
    ritual. The danger is numbers, the dark, the space, and his lungs.
  - **Strong** (a crowd, a trained fighter, someone he knows): beats that
    escalate, and real danger.
  - **Boss** (a Herrscher): the full shape. Escalation, real danger, a
    finisher with ritual that doesn't reuse an earlier move, and a quiet
    human ending for whoever loses.
- Opponents who can learn (a Herrscher, a trained human) may answer his
  tricks. Fodder doesn't. Don't force it.

## 4. Power honesty

- What anyone does matches their kit and [power-scale.md](power-scale.md). A
  move having a name doesn't make it big: [Field Craft: Deer Cut] is a cut to
  the back of a knee.
- **Eros is weak through the Mei run.** Kiana carries the fights; he
  contributes small, real things. No team combos with Kiana, no beating
  anything stronger than one turned adult, nothing his eighteen-year-old
  body couldn't do.
- **His lungs are a limit, not a refrain.** Show it once to set it up, then
  let his weakness show in other ways (shaking hands after, a safety he
  misses, Kiana hauling him up a stair). Never the same line twice ("lungs
  closed like a fist", "ground glass").
- **The right tool.** A fighter uses the best thing they actually have for
  the moment. If his hands know the knife and the spare pistol is in his
  belt, he doesn't run.
- **Reactions to new skills.** When someone shows a skill the others haven't
  seen, show the others react (Kiana: "WHAT was that?").

## 5. Hole checklist (before and after drafting)

- [ ] Does Eros do something offensive that he could actually do?
- [ ] Is anything anyone does beyond their kit?
- [ ] Is the shape right for the enemy (fodder short, bosses full)?
- [ ] In a boss fight, is a big move reused in the finisher?
- [ ] Do names only appear where the POV character could see or hear them?
- [ ] Does every move that matters have a visual and a sound, and the
      biggest an impact frame?
- [ ] Do weapons and devices still work (Kiana's rounds, the radar's
      spring, the box cutter)?
- [ ] Does each fighter use the best tool they actually have?
- [ ] Is any limit, image or line repeated from an earlier fight?
- [ ] Does anyone who sees a new skill react to it?
- [ ] Is every character where the last scene left them?
- [ ] Do later scenes match what the fight left behind (wounds, torn
      clothes, broken things)?
